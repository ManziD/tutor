Is One Month Enough for an Academic Comeback?

By Manzi Delick · Kampala, Uganda

Every year, I meet students who ask the same question as the final examinations draw closer:

“Teacher, is one month enough to improve my grades?”

My answer is always the same: Yes — but only if that month is used intentionally.

One month is not enough to learn everything from the beginning. It is not enough to undo months of neglect through endless reading or sleepless nights. But it is enough to make meaningful progress if you focus on the right things.

As teachers, we often remind our learners that success is not determined by how much time remains, but by how wisely that time is used.

The Mock Exam Is Not the Final Verdict

Many students become discouraged after receiving their mock examination results. Some conclude that they are simply “not good enough.”

That couldn't be further from the truth.

Mock examinations exist for one primary purpose: to reveal your current level of preparation. They answer important questions:

  • Which topics are consistently causing difficulty?
  • What types of questions are you losing marks on?
  • Are your mistakes caused by lack of knowledge, poor exam technique, or careless errors?
  • Is time management affecting your performance?

A mock examination is a diagnostic tool, not a prediction of your final result.

The students who improve the most are rarely those who scored the highest initially. They are the ones who honestly evaluate their weaknesses and commit themselves to improving them.

Stop Trying to Cover Everything

One of the biggest mistakes candidates make during the final month is attempting to revise the entire syllabus equally. This usually leads to panic, exhaustion, and shallow learning.

Instead, ask yourself: “Which topics will give me the greatest improvement if I master them now?”

Focus first on:

  • Frequently examined topics
  • Concepts you almost understand but still struggle to apply
  • Questions you repeatedly answer incorrectly
  • Areas where a small improvement could significantly increase your marks

Progress comes from prioritisation, not perfection.

Quality Beats Quantity

Studying for ten hours a day means very little if your mind is wandering. Three focused hours can accomplish more than an entire day of distracted revision.

Effective revision should include:

  • Practising past papers
  • Reviewing mistakes carefully
  • Timing yourself under examination conditions
  • Explaining concepts aloud
  • Revising actively instead of merely rereading notes

Learning happens when you engage with the material — not when you simply look at it.

Build Confidence Through Small Wins

Confidence is not something that appears on examination day. It is built every time you solve a question you couldn't solve yesterday.

Every corrected mistake. Every completed paper. Every difficult topic you finally understand. These become evidence that you are improving.

Confidence grows from preparation.

One Month Can Change More Than You Think

I've watched students move from average to outstanding. I've seen learners who failed their mocks eventually excel in the final examinations.

Not because they were naturally gifted. But because they stopped wasting energy worrying about the time they had lost and started making the best use of the time they still had.

One focused month can sharpen understanding. It can improve examination technique. It can strengthen confidence. It can completely change the direction of your academic journey.

A Final Word to Every Candidate

If you're reading this, don't ask whether it's too late.

Ask yourself: “What is the best use of today?”

The answer to that question, repeated consistently over the next four weeks, matters far more than the number of days left on the calendar.

Remember, examinations are won by those who prepare with purpose.
